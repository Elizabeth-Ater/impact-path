What does each pillar actually mean?

A closer look at what each Global Citizenship & Goals pillar covers — and why it matters for your first year.

Education

Education is the multiplier goal — it's tied to nearly every other outcome that matters, from lifetime earnings to civic participation to health literacy. Around the world, over 250 million children and youth are still out of school, and many more attend without ever learning to read. This pillar is about closing that gap: expanding access, improving quality, and making sure a good education isn't determined by zip code or income.

Health

Health is the goal that makes every other goal possible — it's hard to learn, work, or lead if basic health needs aren't met. Billions of people still lack access to essential health services, mental health support remains chronically underfunded, and preventable illness continues to shorten lives that better systems could save. This pillar is about building health systems that reach people before a crisis hits, not just respond after one.

Climate Change

Climate change reshapes every other goal on this list — it deepens poverty, disrupts education, and worsens health outcomes for the communities least responsible for causing it. Rising temperatures, extreme weather, and resource scarcity aren't distant threats; they're already reordering how and where people can safely live. This pillar is about the systems-level action needed to slow that trajectory, and the community-level resilience needed to withstand what's already locked in.

Women Empowerment

Progress on every other goal moves faster when women have equal access to education, health care, economic opportunity, and leadership — and stalls when they don't. Despite decades of progress, women remain underrepresented in leadership, underpaid for equal work, and disproportionately affected by poverty and violence. This pillar is about closing those gaps directly — and recognizing that empowering women accelerates all three of the other pillars, too.
